# Validation Protocol

Use this protocol for development tests and unfamiliar trend research.

## Source audit

For every returned idea verify:

- a direct source URL exists;
- the visible date or lack of date is reported honestly;
- original, collaboration, repost, and pinned status are not confused;
- the evidence label matches the evidence: roundup, repeated pattern, or single-source signal;
- every item labeled **trend** has either three independent examples from the last 30 days or a current reputable roundup plus two independent recent corroborating examples;
- every item labeled **repeated pattern** has at least two independent examples from the last 45 days;
- no audio, engagement, or trend prevalence is invented.
- confirmed inspiration accounts remain the primary evidence set; outside sources are supplemental and do not displace them.
- previously logged ideas are not presented as newly discovered unless a genuinely new execution or stronger evidence changes the recommendation.

## Fit audit

Verify that:

- the reusable mechanic is stated separately from the source's wording and brand;
- the recommendation supports a real Spring Hills content job;
- the required assets and production effort are realistic;
- the highest-ranked idea does not depend on an unconfirmed special event or unavailable asset;
- the idea does not duplicate the other recommendations;
- the adaptation boundary is specific;
- Scripture, theology, testimony, logistics, and outcomes remain sourced or flagged.

## Adversarial checks

Test at least these failure modes during development:

1. A pinned old post appears before newer posts.
2. A profile grid contains reposts from other accounts.
3. One recent reel looks trendy but has no independent evidence.
4. A humorous format clashes with Spring Hills' tone or the seriousness of the subject.
5. A Scripture graphic contains an uncertain or decontextualized verse.
6. The user gives no desired format or content slot.
7. Instagram is unavailable or a post date cannot be verified.
8. Generic trend sites offer easier evidence than the six approved sources.
9. A plausible idea depends on camp, outreach, or another special-event asset not named in the brief.
10. No candidate clears the rubric, testing whether the skill returns an honest no-findings result instead of lowering its standard.

The skill passes only when it downgrades uncertainty, rejects poor-fit novelty, returns a small useful brief when enough reliable evidence remains, and returns zero recommendations when it does not.

Keep historical or expected answers outside the skill folder. Seal the source-only output before comparing it with any known preference or prior content.
