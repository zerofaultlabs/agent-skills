# Approved and observed examples

Use these as pattern references, not fill-in-the-blank templates. Do not copy wording when the subject differs.

## Confirmed editorial choices

Nastia confirmed all three rules below through direct feedback:

1. Prioritize the central biblical truth and useful application over a memorable but secondary story.
2. Prefer a small, actionable takeaway over summarizing every sermon point.
3. Move from the human problem toward Christ, Scripture, and a hopeful response.

## Representative caption patterns

### Misconception to application

“We often think contentment is waiting on the other side of better circumstances. But Paul reminds us that it can be learned in every season, whether in abundance or in need.”

Pattern: name a common assumption, correct it with the sermon truth, then offer practical application.

### Changing circumstances to unchanging Christ

“Life will have highs and lows. Plenty and need. Joy and hardship. But when everything around you changes, Christ doesn’t.”

Pattern: use short contrasts, then anchor the caption in Christ's character and presence.

### Three-action reminder

“This is your reminder to pause, pray, and trust God with what you’ve been worrying about.”

Pattern: reduce the response to a few memorable verbs.

### Direct question and biblical example

“How do you respond when life doesn’t go the way you planned? Paul was in prison, yet instead of focusing on his circumstances, he focused on how God was using them to advance the Gospel.”

Pattern: ask a relevant question, ground it in Scripture, then connect it to the reader.

### Gospel contrast

“The gospel doesn’t tell us to work our way to God. It tells us that God has come to us—and He is still working in us.”

Pattern: correct performance-based thinking with grace and God's action.

### Wisdom hook

“Money is a good servant, but a poor master.”

Pattern: use a compact contrast, then explain the deeper spiritual alternative.

### Weekend recap structure

1. Celebrate what happened.
2. Express gratitude.
3. Add one sermon takeaway or spiritual encouragement.
4. Invite people to return when appropriate.

## Selection reminder

A vivid story should support the central biblical truth and useful application. Memorability alone is not editorial importance. Keep case-specific historical comparisons in the external evaluation record.
