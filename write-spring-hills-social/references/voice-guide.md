# Spring Hills social voice guide

## Voice

Write as a welcoming church speaking to real people in everyday life.

- Warm and encouraging
- Upbeat without forced excitement
- Bible-grounded without sounding academic
- Clear and practical
- Hopeful and Christ-centered
- Community-oriented and invitational
- Confident without overstating

Prefer natural second-person language: `you`, `we`, `us`, and `together`.

## Core writing patterns

### Openings

Use one of these when it fits the source:

- A concise misconception correction: “Contentment isn’t something you find. It’s something you learn.”
- A direct question: “How do you respond when life doesn’t go the way you planned?”
- A memorable contrast: “Money is a good servant, but a poor master.”
- A timely reminder: “This is your reminder to pause, pray, and trust God with what you’ve been worrying about.”
- Celebration or gratitude for recap posts.
- A felt-need invitation followed quickly by concrete details.

Do not manufacture tension or use vague clickbait.

### Body

- Develop one idea in one to three short paragraphs.
- Connect teaching to ordinary concerns: anxiety, relationships, purpose, belonging, hardship, faith, or next steps.
- Favor a few concrete verbs over a list of abstract concepts.
- For difficult subjects, acknowledge the tension briefly and move toward supported hope and response.
- Avoid retelling source material that adds no value beyond the video or carousel.

### Closings

Choose no more than one primary close:

- A concise Scripture quotation or reference
- A practical spiritual response
- A warm invitation to a service or event
- A relevant registration or next-step instruction
- Gratitude and a return invitation after a recap
- A simple comment or sharing prompt when participation is the actual goal

Avoid generic engagement bait.

## Formatting

- Keep paragraphs short and mobile-friendly.
- Use sentence case unless the asset specifically needs a display headline.
- Use contractions when natural.
- Use exclamation points selectively; do not make every sentence excited.
- Use roughly zero to two emojis in a typical caption. Place them where they reinforce tone or direction, often near the opening, close, or CTA.
- Use hashtags only when the brief or existing campaign requires them.
- Preserve official capitalization for ministry and event names.

## Format guidance

### Sermon carousel

- Cover: one clear claim, contrast, question, or misconception correction.
- Slides: explain or apply one idea progressively.
- Caption: introduce the theme, name its relevance, and invite the swipe.

### Sermon reel

- Center one emotionally and theologically complete moment.
- Caption: state the takeaway directly; add Scripture or a viewing invitation only when useful.
- Do not duplicate a companion carousel's full treatment.

### Weekend recap

- Lead with celebration, gratitude, or community.
- Add one concise sermon takeaway when relevant.
- Close with encouragement or a natural invitation to return.

### Event invitation

- Lead with the benefit, felt need, or warm invitation.
- Give exact logistics clearly.
- Avoid making routine attendance sound artificially urgent.

### Event recap

- Lead with genuine energy and gratitude.
- Name the people or shared moment that made it meaningful.
- Add a next opportunity only when current and relevant.

## Avoid

- Corporate marketing jargon
- Long theological exposition in a short caption
- Unsupported promises about what God will do
- Invented quotations or paraphrases presented as quotations
- Repeating a sermon anecdote solely because it was memorable
- Excessive emojis, exclamation points, hashtags, or calls to action
- Shame, pressure, fear tactics, or insider language that excludes newcomers
- Treating every post as an event promotion
