# Editorial selection

## Authority levels

Apply confirmed rules as requirements. Use observed rules as strong defaults. Treat low-evidence patterns as optional hypotheses.

### Confirmed by Nastia

1. Prioritize the central biblical truth and useful application over a memorable but secondary anecdote.
2. Compress the sermon into one clear takeaway or one to three memorable actions instead of summarizing every point.
3. Move from the human problem toward Christ, Scripture, and a hopeful response.

### Observed in published content

- Use a supported question, contrast, or misconception correction as the hook.
- Let carousels teach while reels focus on one application.
- Address the audience with encouragement rather than narrating the transcript.
- Let a weekend recap carry one concise sermon takeaway without turning the recap into a sermon summary.

### Needs more evidence

A self-contained opening, transition, or pastoral application may outperform the formal main outline. Use it when the source supports the choice, but present it as a recommendation rather than a learned fact about Nastia.

For reel selection, scan these moments deliberately instead of treating them as exceptions. Score reel candidates independently from the carousel. A faithful, emotionally complete gospel application can outrank the formal outline when it has a clearer felt need, Christ-centered resolution, and complete 60–90 second arc.

Require the reel shortlist to contain the strongest direct gospel or Christ-centered reassurance in the sermon. If the top outline-based reel teaches substantially the same idea as Tuesday, apply the duplication penalty and prefer the strongest faithful alternate that gives the week a distinct emotional or application role.

## Candidate rubric

Score each dimension from 0 to 3. Use scores to expose reasoning, not to replace judgment.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Biblical centrality | Aside | Supporting detail | Important subpoint | Sermon’s central truth |
| Audience relevance | Unclear | Narrow | Relatable | Immediate felt need |
| Useful response | None | Abstract | One clear response | Memorable and practical |
| Christ/Scripture/hope | Absent | Implied | Clear | Essential to the moment |
| Self-contained | Needs long setup | Some repair | Minor setup | Complete as-is |
| Format fit | Weak | Possible | Good | Native to the format |
| Freshness | Repeats companion | Similar | Distinct angle | Adds a complementary job |

Apply these penalties:

- minus 3: anecdote is memorable but not necessary to the central truth;
- minus 3: wording, attribution, timestamp, or theology cannot be verified;
- minus 2: the moment ends in fear, conflict, anxiety, or failure without the sermon’s supported hope;
- minus 2: the candidate substantially duplicates the companion post;
- minus 1: lengthy setup is needed before the clip makes sense.

Do not publish the numeric score unless it helps the user compare close candidates. Always give a plain-language rationale and note why the strongest rejected candidate lost.

## Format decisions

### Carousel

Choose when the idea benefits from progression, explanation, a short list, a misconception correction, or multiple connected Scripture-grounded steps. Keep one central claim across the slides.

For relational, corrective, or problem-heavy subjects, prefer a positive gospel foundation on the cover when it is as clear and compelling as the diagnosis. Let later slides name the conflict or failure. Do not make the audience pass through shame or dysfunction before showing what Christ has done.

Default the cover to the positive gospel foundation for these subjects. Use a diagnostic question only when it contains or immediately signals the hopeful direction; do not make conflict, disunity, or failure the headline when a concise Christ-centered foundation is available.

### Graphic

Choose when one concise, source-faithful line can carry the idea and additional slides would dilute it. Label a paraphrased headline as graphic copy, not a pastor quote.

### Reel

Choose a continuous 60–90 second passage when possible. The opening must make sense without hidden setup, and the ending must land the truth or response. A reel candidate that requires stitched fragments is secondary and must be labeled as an edit concept.

### Weekend integration

Use only when requested or when a supplied weekend recap is part of the assignment. Keep celebration and community primary, then add one sermon takeaway.
