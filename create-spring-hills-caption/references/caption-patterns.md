# Caption patterns by post type

Use these as structures, not fill-in-the-blank copy.

## Photo-based service or community post

- Lead with belonging, gratitude, or a concise observation supported by the images.
- Name the shared moment without identifying people who were not identified by the user.
- Add one warm line about worship, community, encouragement, or being together when supported.
- Close with gratitude or a gentle return invitation.

## Weekend invitation

- Lead with a warm benefit, felt need, or simple invitation.
- Include exact supplied service details cleanly.
- Use one action: come, bring a friend, plan a visit, or join us.
- Avoid artificial urgency or promising a life-changing experience.

## Event invitation

- Lead with a safe felt need or benefit before the event name and logistics. Derive it from verified activities and purpose when the brief does not state it explicitly—for example, a midweek opportunity to slow down, worship, and be together. Phrase it as an invitation, not a promised outcome.
- State exact logistics and registration details from the brief.
- End with one direct invitation.
- If logistics are incomplete, omit optional details or flag the required fact.

## Event recap

- Lead with genuine celebration or gratitude.
- Name the event and people involved only when supplied.
- Highlight one shared moment or purpose.
- Add the next opportunity only when current and relevant.

Short, energetic recaps are appropriate for highly visual youth or children’s events, but do not infer spiritual outcomes from photos.

## Weekend recap with sermon takeaway

- Celebrate the gathering or community first.
- Add one concise, source-supported sermon truth or response.
- Close with encouragement, gratitude, or a return invitation.
- Do not summarize the whole sermon.

## Reel caption

- State or sharpen the reel’s one takeaway.
- Add a practical response, Scripture, or viewing invitation only when supported.
- Do not transcribe the video or repeat all on-screen words.
- Do not present a polished paraphrase as a pastor quote.

## Carousel or graphic caption

- Complement the visual with relevance or context.
- For a carousel, give a clear reason to swipe without explaining every slide.
- For a graphic, briefly develop the headline instead of repeating it verbatim.

## Holiday or cultural moment

- Name the occasion naturally.
- Connect it to faith only when the connection is supported and not forced.
- Verify Scripture and avoid implying the holiday itself carries a theological promise.

## Appreciation or birthday

- Name the person only from the brief.
- Use specific supplied qualities rather than generic praise when available.
- Do not add unsupplied leadership, care, biography, tenure, or impact claims merely to lengthen the caption.
- After the supplied qualities, move directly to the celebration or comment CTA; skip generic filler such as “we hope you feel celebrated” unless the user requested it.
- A comment CTA is appropriate when celebration and participation are the goal.

## Testimony or baptism

- Use only approved details and exact quotations.
- Center God’s grace and the person’s stated experience without embellishment.
- Ask for authoritative wording or approval if the brief is incomplete.
